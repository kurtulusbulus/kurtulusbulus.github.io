My research interests lie at the intersection of biomedical imaging, photonics, engineering and computational methods. I am particularly interested in how measurement hardware and data analysis can be developed together to improve biological and biomedical imaging.

## Biomedical imaging

I am interested in imaging technologies that can capture weak, fast or otherwise challenging biological signals. This includes questions around sensitivity, temporal resolution, signal quality and the practical design of imaging systems.

## Photonics and optical instrumentation

A major part of my work involves optical measurement systems and photodetection. I am interested in how detector characteristics, optical design and acquisition strategies influence the quality and interpretability of biomedical measurements.

## Computational methods

I also work with computational approaches for analysing and reconstructing imaging data, including signal processing, physical modelling and machine-learning methods. I am particularly interested in methods that incorporate knowledge of the underlying measurement process rather than treating imaging data as an abstract numerical input.

## Long-term direction

My long-term goal is to develop imaging technologies in which instrumentation, physical modelling and computational analysis are considered together. I am especially interested in research that connects engineering with neuroscience and biomedical applications.
