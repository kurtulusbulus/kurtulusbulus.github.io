I am a postdoctoral researcher in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Glasgow. My research sits at the intersection of photonics, biomedical imaging and computational methods, with a particular focus on single-photon avalanche diode (SPAD) sensors, high-speed neural imaging, time-resolved spectroscopy and photon-limited reconstruction.

I completed my PhD in Neuroscience at the University of Edinburgh, where I developed and evaluated SPAD-based imaging approaches for fast optical measurements of neural activity. This work included detector modelling, fiber photometry, high-speed imaging and software development for real-time acquisition and analysis.

Before my PhD, I completed an MSc in Informatics with a focus on neuroinformatics at the University of Edinburgh. My dissertation applied machine-learning methods to large-scale extracellular neural recordings. Earlier research experience included machine learning, image processing, simulation and Internet-of-Things systems.

My current work at the University of Glasgow focuses on time-gated Raman spectroscopy and imaging, including optical instrumentation, fluorescence-background suppression and the use of fast photon-counting detectors. Across these projects, I am interested in combining hardware, physical modelling and computational inference rather than treating them as separate problems.

## Research interests

- SPAD and photon-counting imaging
- Biomedical optics and optical instrumentation
- High-speed neural imaging and photometry
- Time-resolved Raman spectroscopy
- Computational and physics-informed imaging
- Self-supervised denoising for photon-limited data

## Background

My training spans electronics engineering, machine learning, neuroinformatics and neuroscience. That multidisciplinary route has shaped the way I approach imaging problems: from detector physics and acquisition hardware through to signal processing, modelling and reconstruction.

Outside research, I enjoy training at the gym, exploring coffee shops and travelling.
